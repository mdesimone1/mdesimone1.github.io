Pioneer Woman Chili Mac

Servings: 6 servings Source: thepioneerkitchen.com

Ingredients

1 yellow onion

2 cloves garlic

1 Tbsp olive oil

1 lb. ground beef

2 Tbsp all-purpose flour

1 Tbsp chili powder

1/2 tsp smoked paprika

1/2 tsp garlic powder

1/2 tsp dried oregano

8 oz. tomato sauce

3 cups beef broth

1/2 lb. uncooked macaroni (about 2 cups)

1 cup shredded cheddar

Directions

Dice 1 yellow onion and mince 2 cloves of garlic. In a large pot or skillet, heat 1 tablespoon of olive oil over medium heat. Add the diced onion and minced garlic. Cook until the onion is translucent and the garlic is fragrant, about 2-3 minutes.

Add 1 pound of ground beef to the pot. Cook, breaking it apart with a spoon, until the beef is fully browned and no longer pink.

Sprinkle 2 tablespoons of all-purpose flour over the cooked beef. Add 1 tablespoon of chili powder, 1/2 teaspoon of smoked paprika, 1/2 teaspoon of garlic powder, and 1/2 teaspoon of dried oregano. Stir to combine and cook for about 1 minute, allowing the spices to become fragrant.

Pour 8 ounces of tomato sauce and 3 cups of beef broth into the pot. Stir well to combine all the ingredients, making sure to scrape up any bits stuck to the bottom of the pot.

Stir in 1/2 pound (about 2 cups) of uncooked macaroni. Bring the mixture to a boil, then reduce the heat to low. Cover and simmer, stirring occasionally, until the macaroni is tender, about 10-12 minutes.

Once the macaroni is cooked, turn off the heat. Stir in 1 cup of shredded cheddar cheese until it’s melted and evenly distributed throughout the dish.

Serve the Chili Mac hot, optionally garnished with additional cheese, fresh herbs, or a dollop of sour cream.

Notes

Lean Ground Beef: Use lean ground beef for less fat. If using a higher fat content beef, you might want to drain the excess fat after browning to keep the dish from being too greasy.

Cheese Varieties: Experiment with different types of cheese for topping or mixing into the Chili Mac. A mix of cheddar and Monterey Jack or even a sprinkle of Parmesan can add depth to the flavor.

Simmering Time: Keep an eye on the macaroni as it cooks. Overcooking can lead to mushy pasta. Taste test a piece of macaroni near the end of cooking time to ensure it’s perfectly al dente.

Stirring: Stir the mixture occasionally while it simmers to prevent the macaroni from sticking to the bottom of the pot and to ensure even cooking.

Thickening: If the Chili Mac is too liquidy for your taste, let it simmer uncovered for a few additional minutes after the pasta is cooked. This allows some of the liquid to evaporate and the sauce to thicken.
